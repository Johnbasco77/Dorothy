**#Introduction**

* After Oppenheimer and Black ( 2024 Tamil Movie), Dorothy is another movie that made me look at cinema through a scientific lens. While the first two movies made me think about the complexities of Quantum physics, Dorothy made me think about something perhaps even more complex like human identity, gender and the biases of the human mind.



**#Story**

* Dorothy is the story of Subbu, who grows up in a family ruled by a cruel and dominating father. His father is deeply casteist, believes strongly in male superiority, and uses violence to control his family. His brutality eventually takes the lives of Subbu’s mother, sister and sister-in-law, Dorothy.
* Growing up in such a house, Subbu has another struggle happening silently within him. From a young age, he begins to realize that he feels like a woman inside. But in a family where even women are not allowed to live freely, Subbu has no courage to express this. Then his sister-in-law Dorothy comes into his life. She understands Subbu in a way nobody else does. One of the most touching scenes is when she tells him, “Your father and brothers suppress the women outside. But you are suppressing the woman inside you.”



* She gives Subbu something he had probably never experienced before — the feeling that it is okay to be who he really is. But the journey is not easy. When Subbu expresses his gender identity to Karthi, his dear childhood friend, even Karthi finds it difficult to accept and starts moving away from him. That part felt very real to me. Acceptance can be difficult even for people who love us. Eventually, Karthi understands and accepts Subbu as a woman. And then comes the beautiful climax. Subbu is given a new name "Dorothy",  in memory of the sister-in-law who first understood the woman within him and gave him the courage to accept himself. 
* For me, that is what makes the title so meaningful. Dorothy saw the woman inside Subbu before the world could see her. And finally, Subbu becomes Dorothy.

In the movie veedu, that same BGM would have been played as a old Fatherly person enjoys the inner beauty as he visits the newly getting constructed house and in Dorothy, the same BGM when Subbu realises the femininity in him! what a brilliance!! **#Realization**

* Glad I watched Dorothy. It helped me notice the log in my own eye before pointing at the speck in someone else’s.



**#Change**

* In the past, I have had harsh feelings towards gender-diverse people, especially when I encountered them at traffic signals and other public places.  
* Watching Dorothy made me reflect on that behaviour and recognize the bias within me. I genuinely regretted it. The movie helped me look at them with greater understanding and, more importantly, look within myself without prejudice.



**#Climax**

* On my first watch, I questioned the climax. The first time I watched Dorothy, I enjoyed the movie and was disappointed by some of the negative reactions towards its LGBTQ+ theme. Yet, even I felt that the director could have avoided the climax where Karthi realises and accepts Subbu, now Dorothy, as a woman and kisses her.
* On my second, the climax questioned me. My second watch changed my perspective. I began to understand why Karthik Subbaraj may have chosen a kiss instead of stopping with a comfortable hug. A hug would have been easier for many of us to accept. But perhaps that was exactly the point. Karthi has loved Subbu as his dear friend for years. When he finally accepts Dorothy as the woman she is, the kiss takes that acceptance beyond friendship — he is not merely accepting her gender identity; he is accepting his love for her.
* Had the film ended with just a hug, perhaps we would all have walked away comfortably. The kiss forced people like me to confront why that extra step made us uncomfortable in the first place. Maybe it would have taken another decade, or another responsible filmmaker, to ask that uncomfortable question.



**#AIContext**

* This is where Dorothy unexpectedly connected with my professional world. We talk a lot about building unbiased, responsible and ethical AI systems. We worry about biased training data, fairness, guardrails and responsible outcomes.
* But a thought disturbed me: How can we expect AI to be free from bias when the humans who build and train it still carry biases of their own?
* I realised I don't have to look at society for an example. I can start with myself. Some of the discomfort I felt during my first watch of Dorothy itself came from biases that I had accumulated over the years. My second watch made me recognise some of them.
* We continuously talk about cleaning and improving the training data of AI. Perhaps, once in a while, we also need to examine our own training data, our upbringing, culture, experiences, assumptions and prejudices.
* Because ultimately, Responsible AI cannot only be about responsible machines. It must also be about responsible humans who design, train and govern them.
